# Using Virtual Cut

A reference for the current workspace controls and behavior, moved from the README on October 3, 2026. Version-specific changes are in the [changelog](../CHANGELOG.md); confirmed product decisions are in [design decisions](design-decisions.md) and [decision records](decisions/README.md).

## Sample media

The local test setup uses six complete 4K AV1 recordings with original audio. Private footage and preview images are excluded from this repository. On a fresh checkout, use **Open video**, or configure the local sample media described in the [workflow preview guide](workflow-preview.md).

## Window and projects

The app launches maximized. Press **F11** or the fullscreen button to hide/show the window frame. The bottom page strip stays visible. **Preview options** in the upper right can reset sample edits or open the earlier Studio/Library/Focus foundation layouts.

Choose **Projects** beside the page name to create a `.vcut` project. Choose its file, finished-clip destination, and a separate preview-cache folder. Use **Import files** or **Import folder** to register completed recordings in the current batch; folder import includes nested folders. Media shows their actual source-folder hierarchy, with descendant filtering and recording counts. Review retains planned clip destinations. Projects save automatically; **Jobs** shows inspection/audio progress, errors, cancellation, and retry. Transcription runs in its own lane, so imports, audio previews and exports start without waiting for it; each lane runs one job at a time, and work you start goes ahead of background work. After a recording's transcript, a background **picture pass** reads every frame once and stores its black screens, still pictures, hard cuts and fades for the Split snap and agents. It uses the NVIDIA GPU decoder while you work and adds CPU workers after 5 minutes without input; without an NVIDIA decoder it runs on the CPU only while the PC is idle. It pauses while a transcription runs or a preview plays and carries on where it stopped. While it reads, the recording's thumbnail shows its progress (for example **42%**) with a thin bar along the bottom, and a tick once its events are ready; hover the tick for the time taken and GPU or CPU. Jobs shows what it is using or why it is paused. The scan icon beside **Show in Explorer** on a recording (**Analyse picture now**), or **Analyse picture now** in Jobs for the whole batch, starts it ahead of other background work under the same rules; use it for recordings imported before this version. Then a **screen text** pass reads one frame a second and stores every piece of text the screen showed (where, when and for how long, never what it means) for agents; it uses the screen text engine on the graphics card and follows the same pause rules. The thumbnail shows its icon and progress while it reads, and both icons with the tick once both are ready. Without the screen text engine the picture pass still runs, the scan icon turns amber, and it and Jobs open **Set up screen text engine** in the Transcript window.

**Transcribe** uses the batch game's names and terms as recognition hints by default whenever the game has any (untick **Use this batch’s names and game terms as recognition hints** to turn them off). Hints run at the normal batch size with a guard: a hinted line that comes back as a stock phrase such as “Thank you for watching!”, or with far too few words for its length, is recognized again without hints and that text is kept. The transcript's summary line says where the hints came from and how many spans the guard redid. Short microphone lines with no voice under them, or stock phrases such as “Thank you.” over a cough, are left out of the transcript, proposals and agent reads; **Original** shows them as **Dropped: no speech**, and **Undo** in the main window brings them back. Heard cues are never dropped, game dialogue is left as heard, and transcripts made before this version are unchanged until transcribed again.

**Heard as** (under a game's names and terms) lists names the transcript keeps spelling wrong: one row per name, with the game's spelling (Cai) and the spellings it is heard as (Kai, Kye). After recognition every whole word that matches, in any case, becomes the game's spelling as an ordinary correction, in game and microphone transcripts: Kai's becomes Cai's, Kaiser is left alone. The transcript, proposals and agent reads say Cai; **Original** shows Kai; **Restore original** on a word or **Undo** in the main window takes it back, and a restored word stays as heard. Your own corrections are never replaced. Each row's game spelling is also sent as a hint. A row works one word for one word, and spoken cue words are never replaced. After you change the rows, the dialog offers **Apply to N transcripts** for this project's earlier transcripts of the game; the summary line of a transcript lists the heard-as spellings used.

**Delete batch…** offers two choices with confirmation and a protective manual save: remove the grouping while keeping work accessible in another batch (or Unbatched), or remove the batch's exclusive app records and disposable previews. Shared recordings and original videos are preserved. Removing app data clears edit Undo history; Save history recovers the project records and regenerates missing previews. Files in use may remain in the cache and are reported.

## Editing, audio and playback

Importing a recording makes no clips: you cut every clip, or accept one from a spoken cue or an agent. A recording with no clips says so in Cut and Review and exports nothing. Accepting a Split (spoken or from an agent) on a recording with no clips makes both clips, from the start to the Split and from the Split to the end, named after the recording unless the card names them; Reopen on the card removes both. **Whole recording** beside the Clips heading in Cut adds one clip from the first frame to the last. In Cut, **Q/W** set the selected clip's in/out points, **S** splits it, and **M** creates and selects a marker. **H · Manipulate** toggles draggable clip edges and circular marker handles. Drag changes a clip edge or marker time while the playhead stays fixed; release commits one undo step, and Escape cancels. Focus a marker and use Left/Right for frame nudges or Shift+Left/Right for about one second. Handles use inspected frame times when available and cannot cross each other or the source boundaries. Click a card or timeline bar to select a clip; selection-follow keeps the last clip selected through gaps. **R** selects the name for fast renaming; **Backspace** requests deletion, **Enter** confirms and **Escape** cancels. Editing shortcuts leave typing fields alone. **Ctrl+Z / Ctrl+Shift+Z** undo/redo and **Ctrl+Up/Down** navigate the active page's items. **Keyboard shortcuts** in the bottom bar provides a reference. The version number at the bottom right opens **What's new**: each version's changes for you and for agent apps, newest first, and which notes each paired agent app has read. After an update the number shows a dot until you open it, and a pop-up offers **See what's new** once.

Batch imports ask about microphone notes and remember audio track assignments. Individual recordings can override them in **Source & audio setup**. Assigned tracks prepare automatically for **Listen: Game / Mic / Combined**, with **Off / Overlay / Replace filmstrip** waveform options beside playback. Waveforms show separate labelled tracks, with amplitude scaled for visibility. Small filmstrip ticks are keyframe positions and can be hidden. Filmstrips are made in the background after import, whenever no other job is waiting, and kept in the project's preview cache: one file per recording, about 12–24 MB per hour of footage. A recording opened before its file is ready shows thumbnails decoded on demand. Volume starts at 100%. Prepared audio is a disposable listening cache; exports will use original source streams. Original video decoding depends on Electron's codec support; reverse playback is a silent seek-based scan.

**K / Space** toggles Play/Pause. **J** scans backward; **L** starts forward at 1× from pause and advances to 2× / 4× / 6× / 8× / 16× on repeated presses. Preview audio plays through 6× and pauses above it. What you hear is lossless: each track's copy keeps the recording's own audio, and ALAC or PCM tracks become FLAC with the same sample rate and bit depth, which is also the audio that exports and filing carry. Reverse uses the same steps and is a silent sampled scan. The active transport highlights teal. **Loop selected clip** repeats its In/Out range and follows the current clip selection; it is disabled without a valid range. Listening choices, audio status, volume and waveforms are grouped on the right of the viewer. The viewer header distinguishes source FPS from measured playback FPS and dropped/total frames. Counts include uninterrupted 1× playback, exclude seeks/loading/scanning, freeze on pause and reset on source changes/reloads. They describe preview playback, not exported media; high-speed preview can skip frames or mute audio.

On **Media**, the left edge has separate folder-tree and media-pool toggles plus a hide/show-both control. Panel widths and visibility persist. **Trash icons on each source card** in Media and Cut remove that recording's batch membership; shared recordings retain their other batches and edits. An exclusive recording's app entries are removed after a protective manual save. Originals, previews and finished exports remain on disk. This operation clears edit Undo; use **Save history** to restore the recording and its edits. The adjacent folder icon opens Explorer at that recording's original file.

## Playback proxies

A proxy is a smaller H.264 copy of a whole recording (1080p, or 720p as a project option) with a keyframe on every keyframe of the recording and at least every half second, and no audio. It has the recording's frames one for one and lives in the project's cache folder; the original is never changed. Nothing is made unless you ask. Each recording card has a proxy chip on its icon row: **+ Proxy** (none), **Proxy 42%** while it is made, **Proxy waiting** or **paused** while it waits its turn, **Proxy 1080p** when ready, **Proxy missing** when its file is gone or changed, and **Proxy failed**. Click it (or focus it and press Enter; arrow keys move, Escape closes) to **Make proxy**, **Stop making proxy**, **Show in Jobs**, **Make proxy again**, **Remove proxy…**, **Show proxy in folder** or **Forget the missing proxy**.

**Proxies** beside Transcript opens the batch's proxy status with **Make proxies for this batch**, and the project's settings: **Make proxies on import** (Off: only when I ask, the default; On: the import step's **Make proxies during import** checkbox starts ticked; Never: hides the chips and the checkbox) and the size. A new size applies to proxies made from then on. Proxies are made in the media lane on the GPU (NVDEC and NVENC, about 7 minutes per footage hour; 1080p takes about 2.3 GB per calm footage hour, 720p about half) and fall back to the CPU without an NVIDIA GPU. Like the picture pass, a proxy pauses while a preview plays or a transcription runs, gives way to work you start, and carries on from where it stopped. Each finished proxy is checked frame by frame against the recording before it is used. Removing a batch with its app data also removes its proxies.

## Export markers

An export starts and ends on keyframes outside the clip, so it can show a moment of the neighbouring clips. Its chapters and `.vcut.json` companion carry only the clip's own markers: a marker in that extra footage belongs to the neighbouring clip and stays out. A range that crosses the clip's edge is cut to the clip, unless less than a second of it falls inside and most of it lies outside; then it is the neighbour's. A marker at the clip's in-point sits at the video's first frame. Exports made before 0.4.30 keep the markers they were written with.

## Clip colors

Each clip card in **Cut** has a **Clip color** list with Resolve's sixteen clip colors (they differ from marker colors). A chosen color shows on the card's swatch and the clip on the timeline; **No clip color** leaves Resolve's default. Exports carry it in the companion, and the Resolve helper's **Apply markers and transcripts** sets it on clips that have no color in Resolve yet. A color you chose in Resolve is never replaced.

## Export subtitles

**Export selected clip** and **File queue** can write **SRT subtitles beside the video** from a finished game dialogue or microphone notes transcript, cut to the clip's verified range: `<video>.srt` and `<video>.mic.srt`, timed from the video's first frame for players. When the exported video carries a start timecode (as camera files and Resolve renders do), each also gets a `.timecode.srt` copy shifted to that timecode, for Resolve's **Audio Transcription > Import from Subtitles**; the companion `.vcut.json` names the copies. Every chosen transcript gets its SRT: when the clip has no speech from it, the SRT is empty and the export result marks it _(no dialogue)_, so a missing SRT always means it could not be written (the result says why). The companion's `subtitles` section names the transcript each SRT holds: game dialogue or microphone notes. The export result lists every file written, or says _No timecode copies: the video has no start timecode_ (OBS recordings carry none). Existing files are never replaced.

## Saving and history

**Save / Ctrl+S** saves immediately and makes a compact manual checkpoint. **Save history** includes autosave settings: a 10-minute default interval and optional **Also save after edits**, both remembered on this device. Automatic saves wait for playback/seeking/manipulation to stop and changes to settle. Normal close saves current work; an unexpected exit can lose edits since the last save. Undo stays in memory across saves and resets on project reopen. Five autosaves and five manual saves are retained in the adjacent `.vcut.saves` folder; restore first protects current work. Existing rolling saves are compacted on upgrade; pre-upgrade copies remain separate. See [save policy and measurements](save-policy.md).

The separate sample workspace still offers **Open video** for a session-only draft. Sample edits use localStorage; real projects use SQLite and do not share the sample scratchpad.

## Agent apps

**Agent** in the header opens Agent access, where agent apps such as Claude Code or Claude Desktop pair with the open project through MCP. **Allow pictures** there (off by default) decides whether they may receive pictures of your recordings. What they read and propose, and how you review their proposals in the transcript window and on the Cut timeline, is in [Agent apps](agent-apps.md).
